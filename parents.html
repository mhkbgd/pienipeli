Our promise

Six things that are true in every game

  • No ads, no purchases

    Nothing to buy and nothing to tap by accident. Every game is free.

  • No sign-up

    No accounts, no emails, no passwords. Just open a game and play.

  • No tracking

    No analytics, cookies or third-party scripts. We know nothing about your child.

  • No losing

    No timers, scores or game-over screens. A “wrong” tap just gets a silly wiggle.

  • Made for ages 1–4

    Simple ideas, big buttons, no reading needed, and a hand that shows how to play.

  • Hard to leave by accident

    Full screen, no zooming during play, and the grown-up buttons tucked into the corners.

Five-minute setup

Make a game feel like an app

Each game can live on your home screen with its own icon. It opens full screen and keeps working without internet.

iPhone & iPad (Safari)

  1. Open the game you want.
  2. Tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  3. Open the game from its new icon: no Safari bars, the whole screen is the game.

Extra safe: turn on Guided Access (Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access). Triple-click the side button in a game and little fingers can’t leave it.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open the game you want.
  2. Tap the menu ⋮, then Install app or Add to Home screen.
  3. Open it from the icon. It runs full screen, with no browser bars.

Extra safe: use App pinning (Settings → Security → App pinning) to keep the screen on the game.

Laptop or computer

  1. Open a game and press the ⛶ button in its corner for full screen.
  2. Every key does something fun, so keyboard bashing is welcome.
  3. Press Esc to leave full screen.

In Chrome or Edge you can also install a game from the install icon in the address bar.

Little lessons

What each game practises

Nothing here is a test. But a lot is going on while they play.

GameAgesWhat they practise
Alphabet Zoo2–4Letter shapes and the sound each word starts with (say “B, bear!” together). Keyboard letters jump straight to that letter.
Build a House2–4Dragging and matching shapes to outlines. Builds a house, a barn and a rocket that blasts off.
Count the Ducks2–4Counting one by one up to 10, seeing how many (the dots) and hearing numbers go up like stairs.
Tap Tap Race2–4Fast tapping, cause and effect, and a race where everybody wins.
Decorate the Cake2–4Free creative play with drag and drop. There’s no right way, and a birthday song at the end.
Letter Pop1–4A first look at letters: pop a balloon, see the letter and its picture. Great for keyboard bashing.
Feed the Numbers2–4Counting out a number of things (1 to 6), matching a number to an amount, and dragging.
Number Train2–4Recognising numbers 1 to 5, matching them, and seeing them in order along the train.
Trace the Letter3–4Pre-writing: following the strokes of each capital letter with a finger. Any order or direction counts.
Tilt Track3–4Steering and hand-eye coordination. No crashing: the grass just slows the car down.
Peekaboo1–3Cause and effect, and the idea that things are still there when hidden (object permanence). A perfect first game.
Animal Band1–4Rhythm, high and low sounds, and playing along with a song. In song mode any tap plays the next note.
Bubble Bath1–4Finger and hand control by rubbing and swiping. A slow, calming game with a happy finish.
Color Splash2–4Colour names (say them together!), making choices, and finishing a picture.
Bedtime2–4Comparing sizes (small, middle, big) and dragging. Ends calm, nice before a nap.
Baby Pop1–4Tapping and swiping, cause and effect. Keyboard bashing counts too.
Find the Animal2–4Listening and first words: the game says an animal’s name and your child finds it among three.
Feed the Animals2–3Matching (monkey and banana, rabbit and carrot) and food words.
Playing together

Six small tips

  1. Sit with them

    Games for this age work best as a shared moment. Your voice is the best part.

  2. Name what you see

    “A red balloon!” “The big bear!” Most games have no words on purpose, so you can add yours.

  3. Keep it short

    A few minutes is plenty. Stop while it’s still fun.

  4. Let them lead

    Mashing the screen is playing too. There’s no wrong way, and no way to lose.

  5. Copy the sounds

    Moo with the cow, hum the lullaby. Little ones love it when you join in.

  6. End calm

    Bedtime and Bubble Bath slow things down. Nice ones to finish with.

FAQ

Questions parents ask

Are Pienipeli games really free?

Yes. Every game is completely free to play. There are no in-app purchases, subscriptions or hidden costs, and there never will be.

Do the games have ads?

No. Pienipeli has no ads at all, so there is nothing for little fingers to tap by accident.

What age are the games for?

Babies and toddlers, roughly ages 1 to 4. Each game shows a suggested age. They need no reading, and every tap does something fun.

Do I need to download anything or sign up?

No. The games run in your web browser on phones, tablets (including iPad) and computers. There is no app to download and no account to create.

Do you collect any data about my child?

No. There are no accounts, analytics, cookies or trackers. A few small settings (like sound on or off and your language) are saved only on your own device.

How can I stop my toddler from leaving the game?

Tap the full-screen button in the game. On iPhone and iPad you can also use Guided Access (Settings → Accessibility → Guided Access) to keep the screen on one game.

Can I put a game on my home screen like an app?

Yes. On iPhone or iPad tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android open the browser menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen. Each game gets its own icon, opens full screen, and works without internet once it has been opened.

Do the games talk or use words?

Only Find the Animal speaks (it asks “Where is the cat?”). The other games use no speech at all: just music, chimes and playful animal sounds. The letter games show short words for grown-ups to read aloud.

How long should my toddler play?

Short and sweet is best: a few minutes together, then off to something else. The games never push to keep playing: no streaks, rewards or “one more level”.
